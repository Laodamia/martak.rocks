💭 Chapter One: Philosophy

Beliefs, principles and values that guide everything else in the codex.

1: Meta-beliefs

  • Your beliefs shouldn’t reflect what sort of person you are, but how the world is.

    All beliefs are subject to change. To generate accurate beliefs we need evidence. Don't resist new evidence, that's the only way to change & grow.

    Keep your identity small. Leave yourself a line of retreat. To make it easier to accept new ideas, accept the possibility of a change of mind.

  • Values are the engines of motivation. Distinguish your true values from your should values.

    If you’re wrong about what you value, you’ll mismanage your motivational resources.

    It’s tempting to pick values based on a belief that holding them will make you better off, but it’s not the same as having them motivate you for real.

2: Personal Principles

Be courageous in expressing your values and living by your principles.

  • Courage is to do the right thing when no one is watching. Sticking to the path you know is right even if it doesn't promise recognition.
    • Don’t negotiate with yourself. Be the person who you strive to become; resist doing the things that are easy, but that violate your principles.
    • Life of meaning demands courage. It's one in which your values will be tested, extended, and expressed.
    • Practice being uncomfortable. Leave your comfort zone regularly. Overcoming small challenges gives perspective and helps focus on more important issues.
  • Your needs and boundaries are important and changeable. Practice (re)defining and expressing them.

Preserve cognitive power to spend it where it matters.

  • Cognitive power is the only currency. Direct it towards what matters. Fixating on your own problems is a form of self-indulgence.
    • Don’t focus on things you can’t control. Learn more from the Stoics. Don't make your feelings dependent on external metrics, but on living by your values.
    • Decisions are tiring. Reducing decision-making costs preserves your bandwidth. Say “no” more often to prevent overwhelm.

Maintain flexibility to be able to continuously self-improve & evolve in the right direction.

  • Flexibility increases happiness. The ability to always pick what you want to work on rather than being stuck in what you have to do gives a sense of self-direction and increases your leverage. Maintain your freedom.
    • Compound interest. Small actions compound over time. This applies to relationships, learning, and business. If you don’t intend to work to improve something, at least don’t make it worse.
    • Cost awareness. Pay attention to the value of our time and when it’s worth spending money to buy more time.

3: Politics and Society

  • Strong opinions weakly held. Have strong justifications for your beliefs, but know what evidence you'd need to see to change them. Always adjust based on new evidence.
    • Be patient in conversations, focus on understanding what others mean, and not alienating them.
      • Definitions are relative. Nothing is binary and labelling leads to prejudice. Always opt to hear a longer explanation about someone’s views instead of sticking to a label. Ask: “what does it mean for you to be a feminist/vegan/anti-natalist/EA?”
      • Don’t alienate. You will likely not convince people of anything in one conversation. Focus on asking questions about the reasons for their beliefs, and at least not alienating them against the concepts you stand for.
      • Patience in discussions. Don't assume people know the definitions of concepts that are familiar to you. Ask if they know them and make sure you know how to explain without impatience.
  • Doing good. You are lucky to be among the 5% of the highest earners in the world. It’s your duty to give back 10% of your income.
  • Cultural relativism doesn’t exist. The right to have an opinion doesn’t make an opinion valid. Encroaching other people’s rights and well-being can’t be excused by following an opinion or chosen lifestyle.
  • Religion is poison. Religious groups/religions are consistently slowing down the progress of humanity— fossilising our views on ethics and actively harming people (physically and mentally).
  • AI safety is the most important area to focus on. Regardless of the speed of take-off scenarios or good vs. bad outcomes, AI will bring unprecedented change in society.
    • Follow the field only as much as it helps with decision-making. Otherwise, fear and overwhelm is likely.

🛠️ Chapter Two: Personal Systems

Systems and tactics for getting the most out of your life.

1: Productivity

  • Plan all tasks. Start a working session, by listing 1-3 main tasks you want to accomplish.
    • Always get out of the way first the thing you least want to do.
    • Give yourself permission to start and not finish a task rather than postpone it.
    • Have an end time to your tasks (I will work on this until X pm); and to your work day.
    • Have a clear plan for every large task: break it down into small pieces. If you feel a lot of resistance against starting it, it's probably because it's not broken down well.
    • List random distracting thoughts and tasks on a piece of paper or in Roam. Complete them in bulk after a working session.
  • Separation work & sleep. When possible, don’t work in the same room you sleep in. If working alone/remotely, occasionally change your workplace (e.g. go to a cafe); while it can somewhat reduce productivity it will increase motivation.
  • Silence. Keep your workspace quiet. You have the right to tell other people to turn their phones or computers down, especially in shared workspaces.
  • Take breaks. Ending a day with a “fried brain” is not a sign of success but of mental mismanagement. Dance or stretch in breaks. ****Aim to end a work day with enough mental energy to be able to read a book.

2: Food

  • Food is one of the things that takes a higher-than-average amount of your bandwidth. But, it doesn't have to control you. Use rules & systems to prevent anxiety.
    • Don’t waste time on food decisions. You can eat the same thing every day. If others don't cook for you, cook simple things in bulk.
    • Always carry some food with you when you travel. There is no guarantee you'll find anything you can or want to eat in transit, or that you'd have decision bandwidth for that.
    • Keep easy-to-eat, protein-filled food at hand for sudden hunger (tofu, cans of chickpeas, or beans work well). Always keep fruits or healthy snacks around for evening hunger.
    • Having a set time for an evening meal makes it easier to structure the day and prevent overeating.
    • Drink a lot of fruit tea or water during the day. Always keep a full cup/bottle at your desk. Otherwise, you'll forget, and being hydrated keeps you more focused.
  • Food can be an expression of ethics.
    • Everyone should strive to reduce their meat consumption. Encouraging reducetarianism in more people is more efficient that convincing one person to become vegan.
    • Lead by example, not by angry preaching.
  • Food is tightly connected with your emotional state.
    • Meals with people are a learned skill. They can be stressful, but in a controlled safe setting can also add evidence to the assertion you can eat normally, and prevent you from overeating.
      • Never force yourself to participate, if it causes too much distress. In the end, nobody cares what you do.
      • To diminish meal distress: try to learn when a meal starts and how long it will last. Set yourself a time at which you’ll leave the table, to prevent reaching a point of no return in your anxiety.
      • Sitting at a table filled with unfinished food is likely to cause mindless eating. Leave the table if you can or suggest further social interactions to take place at a different location.
    • Stop before eating and think about your reasons. Are you really hungry or are there other causes (pleasure, socializing, habit, boredom, sadness, etc.)? Ask your future self how she'd feel about this decision.
    • Don’t watch YT, read, or scroll on the phone while eating. Listen to meditation music to keep in touch with your body’s needs and to make mindful decisions about what and how much you consume.
  • Food choices are your right, there is no need to be ashamed of them.
    • Everyone has their own preferences, there is also a multitude of ways to be peculiar about food that are culturally accepted. In the safety of their own home, everyone is “weird” about food.
    • Standing up to your preferences can even be inspiring to others.
    • Being ashamed of what you eat increases anxiety.
    • The number of people who use food to handle their emotions is higher than you anticipated. You only see or remember the most extreme examples, but there are more which are less extreme but still potentially “weird”.

3: Physical Health

Treat your body as a friend, not as a vessel to achieve objectives.

  • Always incorporate physical activity into your day, ideally weight training. It makes you feel strong and increases focus.
    • Weights/strength training are always preferable to cardio. It's for your body and self-confidence and, for your future health.
    • Stretching during the day or in the evening usually seems aversive, but makes your body feel good.
  • Take appropriate supplements: Vit D (~3000IU), B12 (min 300 mcg), and 20-25mg of zinc for the scalp.
    • For a cold: At the start of a cold double the amount of zinc (take morning & evening) and increase the amount of vit D to 10000 IU (split morning & evening). This has a chance to prevent the cold from developing or to reduce its duration.
    • For focus: ketone esters (5-7.5mg/day) are helpful.
    • For mood (and/or focus): Microdosing LSD (10-12.5 mcg every 2-3 days), and 1500-1650 mg Lion’s Mane a day has been helpful.
  • Shampooing hair is unnecessary, it’s enough to wash it with water once a week. Drying with a hairdryer makes your scalp flaky, let it dry on its own. For dry, flaky, or scabby scalp use aloe vera gel, it will soak into the hair too.
  • Organise your day with sleep in mind. Have a sleep routine, at an absolute minimum avoid bright overhead lights 30 min before bedtime.
    • Ideally: cold shower in the morning, only 1 coffee a day (max 12 [pm with wakeup time at 7]), nose plaster for better breathing.
    • If your hips hurt during sleep, change the mattress.

4: Emotional Health

  • Investigate yourself regularly. The algorithms you live by can change.
    • Meditate to hear your automatic thoughts and separate yourself your thoughts and feelings from your actions and identity.
    • Eating is hard for you. Many stories in your head are triggered by eating or sharing meals. Remind yourself that these are just stories.
    • Understanding yourself is for the benefit of others. It can help you protect them from negative reactions and suboptimal behaviours triggered by old stories or produced by following old patterns.
  • Practice gratitude. It's a happiness multiplier.
    • Notice the positive impact of others on your life and let them know in words or actions.
    • Noticing the lack of suffering is a good way to be grateful in the moment.
    • Find things you can thank others for. Positive feelings should not be kept inside: spreading happiness makes you happier. Make gifts, send postcards.
  • Prioritise new experiences over work or habit, but not at all costs. They might require more activation energy but will make you more satisfied in the long term.
    • Do not ignore social life. Interacting with other people, even if not 100% value-aligned ones, is necessary to maintain mental stability.
    • Travel always makes you feel more alive, teaches you new things, and prevents getting complacent. If you feel afraid of leaving a place, it means it's time to go.
    • Give yourself permission not to do CoZe. If you’re feeling unsafe or anxious, your capacity for exploration is much lower.
  • Strive to be more Tell Culture. Other people’s emotions are not your responsibility. Don’t guess or assume how other feels and don’t act based on the needs you anticipate they have.
  • Femininity is not a weakness. You have a strong, nourishing feminine posture in you—practice it, it gives you a sense of groundedness.
    • Femininity doesn’t imply servitude.
    • Notice and appreciate female role models.

💼 Chapter Three: Work

Lessons for business, work, and building things.

1: Career

  • Direct your career towards AI safety. This is both a field that matters and one that will be “job secure” in the long term.
    • Read AI papers and take the time to understand the concepts by taking with GPT. This increases your fluency in the topic helping to communicate with different people and stakeholders.
    • Take time to clarify your reasoning and views on specific aspects of the topic.
  • Keep learning STEM subjects (programming, maths, decision theory, probability).
    • In 10 years you would have regretted not doing it now. It helps increase accuracy of thinking and get a more precise understanding of the world.
    • When learning gets tough, it's when most people drop. This is why this skill is valued. The ability to persevere makes it the skill for you, not any built-in talent you might have.
    • Even if you work in a different position, make using data or learning programming a part of your weekly routine.
  • Have a clear(ish) career plan, but question your goals regularly.
    • Remind yourself how what you do leads to what you want to achieve and readjust if needed. It's easier to prevent doubts from arising than dissipate them once they take hold.
    • Don’t jump at every job opportunity you are given. You have enough skills to be choosy.

2: Business

  • Always pick jobs that you enjoy day to day, and teams that are easy to work with.
    • Day-to-day is what most of the work time is comprised of. "Coolness of the job" or salary will never compensate for an annoying team, repetitive tasks, or lack of autonomy.
    • Suffering in your job just for the sake of the feeling of “overcoming hardship” doesn't add value to the output of your tasks. No one pays for how you feel while doing a task, but only for the job being done.
    • Remember that you can ask for different options of part time (e.g. 1/5, 4/5)
  • Money is not a good business or career goal, but it is necessary to live.
    • Don't deceive yourself or use false modesty to excuse getting a low salary or to justify not earning enough.
    • Have clearly defined “money needs” per quarter to know when you're financially safe. It will prevent you from taking on more jobs when it's not necessary.
  • Be clear in your mind about how your daily tasks help you develop skills you want to or need to learn, for yourself or your future career.
    • Remember, if you have a manager, you can ask for a switch of focus in your role. The worst you'll hear is no.
    • Don't be afraid to have a side project. It doesn't need to bring money (beware of the myth of the hustle!), what it does is it keeps you engaged.

3: Management (of yourself and others)

  • Optimize for increased bandwidth and focus. Pay for it more if necessary.
    • A quiet working space is a must; a silent co-working or a separate room in an apartment. Don't assume you can work from cafes.
    • If you stay somewhere for longer invest in a good chair.
    • Noise-cancelling headphones and listening to techno help with focus.
  • Recognise and avoid fake work. Always make a plan, but by default try to speed-run things and focus on the details at the second pass if possible.
    • Most meetings are useless. Everything can be summarised in a well-written email. Calls should be kept to max. 30 minutes and have an agenda written and shared beforehand.
    • Checking emails and networking are also fake work.
    • Outsource what you know how to do best—you’ll be able to assess the results. Leave for yourself the things you don’t exactly know how to do and want to learn or the things you enjoy.
  • Give people a benefit of doubt. If clients or colleagues annoy you, thank them first for what they did right and how they contributed positively. It makes for better relationships.
  • Keep an updated goals list. Even if you don't follow it 100%, it shows you what you consider important month by month (otherwise you will forget). It's also a clear record of your planning fallacy.

4: Skill Development

  • Learning new skills is the most creative and mind-taxing task. Do it in the morning, before you start work.
  • You’re never too smart to be confused. Admit ignorance quickly.
    • Don’t let the judgements of others (real or perceived) impair your curiosity and learning.
  • Data & quantitative skills
    • Do a little bit of learning STEM subjects or hard reading (e.g. of AIS papers) every day. It's very easy to forget skills and lose the habit.
    • Learning quantitative skills is important for increasing the sharpness of your brain and easier self-debiasing.
  • Reading & writing
    • Write more, it’s a skill you need to practice. Ask experienced others to edit your work.
    • Assign time to read non-fiction, otherwise, it won't happen. Books, papers & long formats are equally mind-taxing, don't expect yourself to be able to read all of these types of texts at the same time.

👨‍👩‍👧‍👧 Chapter Four: People

Beliefs and practices for other people.

1: Strangers

  • Always be as polite and understanding as the person you perceive yourself to be.
    • Everyone is full of insecurities. The way they behave and communicate often reflects that.
    • Use NVC. If you find something uncomfortable, rude, or annoying, start by stating the facts.
    • Leave people happier or better off after talking to you, than when they started. It might require not saying everything you'd like to, but will leave you feeling more peaceful.
    • Call out men if they catcall or whistle. It can be uncomfortable, but you're doing it for other women too. E.g. "I heard you said something to me. What was it? It makes me feel disrespected. Please don't do it again."
  • Most people don't care about the truth. You're unlikely to convince them about anything.
    • Don’t lie. But, don’t deliberately hurt with how you express the truth.
    • The best you can do it to bring others one step closer to understanding a topic. Sometimes the least you can do is not alienating them towards the concepts under discussion or towards other people who share your beliefs.
  • Try to use AR prompts/practices with new people. It helps build deeper relationships faster.
    • Don’t be afraid to share your vulnerabilities first, it helps others open up

2: Friends

  • Most people will not understand you. Invest in those with whom you share values and outlook. They are a source of comfort, security, and positive change.
    • It’ll always be worth travelling to see a friend.
    • Send cards for Christmas or without an occasion.
    • At the least, send an occasional “what’s new?” message, even after a long break.
    • Make time for long chats with Oliver.
  • Never hesitate to ask friends for help. You chose them for shared values and intelligence; collectively they have more expertise than you do.
  • Cultivate only those relationships that make you feel positive and help you be a better person. Be clear to yourself how each relationship makes you feel.
    • Spend more time with people who have qualities you want to improve, and less with those who will enhance the qualities you want to fight. Non-judgemental, open, perceptive, self-aware people are your target.
    • Remember that you might relate strangely to those *who display even one of the behaviours or qualities your parents do. You might end up being unreasonably drawn to them or judgem*ental of them (or both).

3: Family

  • Approach family with the same rules as you would strangers. It helps avoid getting quickly wrapped up in past stories.
    • Family members can surprise you with their attitudes. When in a good mood, give them a chance and try an intelligent discussion like you’d have with friends, try AR.
    • Focus on finding things you agree on. Only comment on contentious issues when explicitly asked.
    • Don’t force yourself to be kind. If they do or say something you wouldn’t let go if it was a friend or a stranger, use NVC to communicate that.
    • Treat meetings as opportunities for anthropological observation and understanding rather than sharing your points.
    • Be honest about your food issues and preferences, you are an adult who can confidently express them and they are adults who can reason (🤞).
  • Encourage a non-material gift culture. To prevent unwanted gifts ask for specific experiences, to spend time together, or to commit to something more valuable than items.
  • Spend time with your mum. You have less time left to spend together than you want to admit to yourself. She deserves your time and to be taken out for quality time.
    • Plan a holiday together at the beginning of the year, if you leave it for later it’s unlikely it will happen.

4: Partnership

  • Not one person can provide you with all that you need emotionally physically or otherwise, and that's ok. You don't have to be ok with all of their behaviours.
    • Make time to inhabit different personas together, to practice accepting each other in different roles (cute, intellectual, elegant, serious).
    • Make a habit of talking about serious, new, or thought-provoking topics. Otherwise, you might miss the moment when you grow apart.
    • Be clear to yourself and share what your needs and boundaries are. What might seem like an imposing/bothersome ask in your head, might be easy for your partner and the reverse.
  • Keep a low threshold and clear up any withholds. Set up a scheduled (weekly) meeting to focus on relationship building.
    • Even the best partners will annoy you sometimes. But, there should not be a topic you can’t discuss with your partner.
    • Share how much bandwidth you have each week/day to clarify who has more capacity to carry the mental load. Ideally, share specifically what support you’d like to get.
    • Going through challenges and new situations together can help strengthen the relationship. Acknowledge to yourselves the level of challenge each situation/decision poses.
  • Notice when you go into posture; resist the temptation to be a mother or a personal coach to partners.
    • Holding any persona for a long time is likely to consume you and destroy your individuality. Note when you have this response, acknowledge it, and explain to yourself why it's not beneficial for you.
    • You’re not in a relationship to prove anything to anyone or to be “useful”.
  • Some people will like you for who you are. It doesn’t imply immediate dependency not requires immediate reciprocity.

5: Physical Contact & Sex

  • Sex is an activity like any other. It doesn’t need to have any extra, deep meaning implied (unless you ascribe it to it). It should be fun.
    • Avoid using sex or other people’s appreciation as tools to boost self-confidence.
    • Don’t treat sex with pathos. In essence, it’s funny—just bodies rubbing and flapping.
  • It's important to be physically close with other people, especially partners. But, no amount of physical closeness should imply sexual activity is the next "expected" step.
    • You don’t owe sex, or any part of the act, to anyone.
    • Sex is not a performance for others. Try not to focus on how you look or how things “should go”, but on how you feel.
  • Seek out platonic physical contact (hugs, cuddles). You need it for your well-being.

    Seek to hang out with people who understand that friendship can include these expressions of affection.

    There are various types of friendships and relationships which can involve different degrees of physical closeness. Physical closeness is not reflective of the strength of the relationship.

🏖️ Chapter Five: Nitty Gritty

Important habits to take care of the details of life.

1: Clothes

  • Reduce the number of clothing items. There is always excess.
  • If buy anything, invest in quality, comfort, and durability.
    • Leggings should always have a pocket that fits a phone.
  • Thin close-to-skin long-sleeved tops are a great under-layer for cold months.
  • Clothes are for self-expression. Don’t restrict your weirdness.

2: Shopping & Gifts

  • Keep a list of items or experiences you might enjoy. It makes it easier to delegate gifts when an occasion comes.
  • Don’t buy things. You don’t need them. Buy things for others only if you know they need them.
  • Choose hand-made gifts or spending time with others over buying items for them. Give gifts without an occasion.

3: Apartments

  • Invest in a comfortable home. This is where you usually spend most of your time and you have too many anxiety triggers to financially compromise on this.
    • The bedroom needs to be quiet and have black curtains. If possible, choose a non-shaky mattress.
    • If both you and your partner or flatmates work from home, you have to work from two separate rooms with two separate workspaces.
    • Personal space is key in shared apartments. You won’t mentally last without having a place to retreat to.
    • When choosing apartments check for good heating in cold countries, aircon in hot ones, and for insulation in both.
    • Buy a small heater if you’re cold, they are just EUR 30-50. Being cold takes too much stress.
    • In the winter in the northern hemisphere make sure you have a lot of light. Buy photography lamps (min 1000 lumens), and turn on all the overhead laps.
  • The ability to take comfortable, non-stress walks is essential. Proximity to nature is preferable.
  • Co-living can be great for preventing isolation. When choosing co-workings pay attention to noise levels.

☹️ Chapter Seven: Anxieties, Worries, and Fears.

  • In my lifetime climate change, a pandemic, or an AI-related catastrophe will turn life into a fight for resources and survival.
  • I will never have enough money for it to stop preventing me from working on personal projects.
  • I will never achieve anything of value, or produce anything of importance. Either because I’m just a regular, unnoticeable, basic person, or because I’m too lazy to push hard enough (because I am just a regular person).
  • It will turn out I am an impostor, living in a fantasy of having something valuable to contribute in terms of talent, skills, knowledge or other qualities.
  • Mum will die and I will not know what to do. I will regret not spending enough time with her.
  • Years of neglecting my ED or my bone and posture problems will result in a serious disease that will make my or others' life unbearable.
  • No one will love me and fully accept me and I’ll feel lonely and misunderstood (again) for most of my life.
  • I’ll make a stupid mistake that destroys my life (mix the wrong psychoactive substances and get into psychosis, get caught carrying a tiny bit of drugs and go to an Asian jail).
  • I'll have an accident and my mind will be trapped in an otherwise motionless body.

🤩 Chapter Eight: Aspirations

(Non-time specific.)

  • Earn six figures (USD) by the age of 40.
  • Don't work more than 4 hours a day (if a full-time version pays 6 figs).
  • Always listen more than speak. Keep practising asking better questions.
  • Make NVC your default form of communication even in stressful situations.
  • Do more art: collages, poetry (even just reading), go to art galleries, make conceptual things even for nobody.
  • Organise more events: solstice, Petrov Day, AR workshops, or cuddle parties.
  • Start a more involved sport, e.g. Muay Thai, bouldering, back to pole dancing.
  • Play drums on stage.
