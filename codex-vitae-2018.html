Chapter One: Philosophy

Beliefs, principles and political values that guide everything else in the codex.

1: Meta-beliefs

  • Personal growth comes from understanding that beliefs are fluid. It is their nature to change — the change is a sign of personal growth.
  • Holding axioms without jusification is unhealthy; leads to close-mindedness and prejudice. The ability to back up and explain our beliefs, helps us feel more grounded and helps in discussions.

2: Personal Principles

  • Freedom is happiness. Ability to realise our ideas gives a sense of self-direction and power. Maintain your freedom.
  • Doing good. You are lucky to live in the 5% of the world with highest income. It is your duty to give back.
  • Compound interest. Even small wins compound over time. This applies to relationships, learning, and business. If you don’t intend to work to improve something, at least don’t make it worse.
  • Decisions are tiring. Reducing decision making cost increases productivity.
  • Cognitive power is the only currency. Direct it only where it matters.
  • Break conventions. Don’t take everything seriously. Establish a parallel universe with your own rules, do weird and unexpected things. It helps maintain curiosity and prevents complacency.
  • Practice being uncomfortable. Do something that stands out, leave your comfort zone. Overcoming small challenges gives perspective and helps focus on more important issues.
  • Don’t focus on things you can’t control. Learn more from the Stoics.
  • Shift your definitions of happiness and success to always feel happy and successful. Don’t make it dependent on others.
  • Don’t negotiate with yourself. Be the person who you strive to become; resist doing things that are easy, but violate your principles.

3: Politics and Society

  • Liberty applies to more people than yourself. Other people can choose for themselves how to live.
  • The right to opinion doesn’t make an opinion valid. Encroaching on other people’s rights and wellbeing can’t be excused by following an opinion or chosen lifestyle.
  • Decentralised systems and technologies are the next step to uninhibited global collaboration. Relying on human-lead systems is unscalabe.
  • AI and bioengineering should be approached with caution — not everyone in the society holds the same views on liberty and equality.
  • Religious groups/ religion are consistently harbouring progress.
  • Nothing is binary (gender, sex, political views) — labelling people leads to prejudice. Always opt to hear a longer explanation about someone’s views rather than stick to a label (ask “what does it mean for you to be….?”)
  • Most people still don’t know what it means to be a feminist. Men must be educated that feminism serves them too.
  • Most people breed for egoistic reasons — need for love, need for future caretakers, lack of hobbies, lack of career priorities, need of power over someone. Lack of character self-awareness will make their children unhappy, without working on improving the world they are sentencing them to a life of potential pain and environmental destruction.

Chapter Two: Personal Systems

Systems and tactics for getting the most out of your life.

1: Productivity

  • Always incorporate sport or physical activity into you day.
  • Start a working session, by listing three main tasks you want to accomplish.
  • Always get the hardest thing out of the way first.
  • Give yourself permission to start and not finish a task rather than postpone it.
  • List random distracting thoughts, and task on a piece of paper. Complete them all after a working session.
  • Have a clear plan for a every major task: break it down into manageable chunks.
  • Have an end time to your tasks (I will work on this until Xpm); and to your work day.
  • Maintain a separation between work and home space.

2: Food

  • Don’t waste time on food decisions: Eat Huel. Carry some around for unexpected hunger when you’re out.
  • Everyone should strive to reduce their meat consumption. Encouraging reducetarianism in more people is more efficient that convincing one to be vegan. Lead by example.
  • Having a set time in the evening for a meal makes it easier to structure a day and prevent overeating.
  • Meals with people are a learned skill. Can be stressful, but as a habit can also teach to eat "normally”/not overeat (comparison with others).
  • Stop before eating and think about your reasons. Are you hungry or is it just for pleasure, for socializing, out of habit, boredom or sadness?
  • Drink a lot of green tea and water during the day. Always keep a full cup/bottle at your desk. Aim for 2-3 litres.

3: Emotional Health

  • Meditate to control your inner life and separate yourself form your thoughts and feelings.
  • Make time for art: collage making makes you happy; likewise poetry — helps digest reality.
  • Practice gratitude. Notice the positive impact of others on your life and tell them at every occasion; whether it’s by words or actions.
  • Don’t compare your inside with other people’s outside.
  • Give random compliments. Positive feelings and thoughts should not be kept inside: spreading happiness makes you happier.
  • Invest only in meaningful relationships, those that make you feel positive. Make time for long chats with Oliver.
  • Spend time with mother: she deserves your time and to be taken out.
  • Travel. Not even money is an excuse. It always makes you feel more alive, better, and teaches you new things.

Chapter Three: Work

Lessons for business, work, and building things.

1: Career

  • Money is not a career goal.
  • Have a job that makes you happy. Most people just blindly follow the prescribed path. Don’t be one of them.
  • Make sure your job helps you develop skills you want to learn, for yourself or for the future career.
  • Have a career plan. Don’t jump at every job you are given. You can be choosy.

2: Business

  • Have your own business/side project. Even if it doesn’t initially bring money it keeps you engaged and motivated.
  • List ideas for business/projects you want to work on later. The time might come.

3: Management (of yourself and others)

  • Do tasks you enjoy or want to learn, outsource the rest.
  • Outsource what you know how to do best — you’ll be able to asses it. Focus on things you don’t know exactly how to do.
  • Most meetings are useless. Everything can be summarised in a well-written email. Skype calls should be kept to max 30 min.
  • You don’t have to be the decider for everything, giving freedom to others gives you freedom.
  • Have principles and lead by example. Ocassionally give specific person-tailored praise.
  • Don’t fool yourself: meetings, email checking, and networking are "fake work".

4: Writing

  • Write more, it’s a skill you need to practice. Ask experienced others to edit your work.
  • You don’t have to start at the beginning, just write what comes to your head. The beginning and end will be filled.
  • Fear of an empty page can be dispelled by pasting a bit of text at the bottom.
  • Remove excessive first person pronouns. They indicate insecurity and make the writing read like non-inclusive bragging.
  • Be cohesive. Remove redundancy. Visual formatting aids processing information.

Chapter Four: People

Beliefs and practices for other people.

1: Strangers

  • Smile. Most people don’t mean harm, even if they are stupid.
  • Always be the polite person you perceive yourself as.
  • Call out men if they cat call you or whistle at you. “What is it that you said? I find it rude. Don’t do it again.”
  • There is always something you can learn from others. Find their passion and them all about it. They will be happy and you will learn something.

2: Friends

  • Know who your close friends are and invest in them. At the least, send an occasional “what’s new?” message.
  • Most people will not understand you. Cherish those who you share something with.
  • It’ll always be worth travelling to see a friend.
  • Send cards for Christmas or without an occasion.
  • Never hesitate to ask friends for help. You chose them for shared values and intelligence; collectively they have more expertise than you do.

3: Family

  • Try not to get wound up by them. Only comment on contentious issues when explicitly asked.
  • Encourage a non-material gift culture: experiences, time, or commitment to something are more valuable than items.
  • They can surprise you with their attitudes, give them a chance. When in good mood, try an intelligent discussion like you have with friends.
  • Don’t force yourself to be kind, if they do something you wouldn’t let fly if it was a friend or a stranger.

4: Partnership

  • Non-monogamy gives freedom. Not one person can provide you all that you need emotionally, and it’s ok.
  • Be clear about what you want from any partner and what you are happy to give them. Establish clear boundaries early.
  • Resist the temptation to be a mother or personal-coach to people. It will consume you and destroy your individuality. Note when you have this response and reject it: it’s not your problem, it’s not your job.
  • Spend time with people who have qualities you want to improve. Not with those who will enhance the qualities you want to fight. Non-judgemental, open, perceptive, self-aware people are your target.
  • There should not be a topic you feel you can’t talk about.
  • Accept that some people will just like you. It doesn’t mean immediate dependency, or requires immediate reciprocity.

5: Sex

  • You don’t owe anything to anyone. Put your pleasure first.
  • It’s not a performance. Don’t focus on how you look, but on the sensations.
  • Physical contact is a way to deal with insecurities and release tension. Don’t fear it.
  • Sex is an activity/hobby like any other. It doesn’t have any extra deep/relationship meaning, unless you ascribe it to it.
  • Avoid using sex or other people as a tool to boosting self-confidence.

Chapter Five: Nitty Gritty

Important habits to take care of the details of life.

1: Clothes

  • Reduce number of clothing items. There is always excess. Clothes shopping is a waste of time.
  • Have a go-to comfortable smart wear: black dress.
  • If buy anything, invest in quality. Especially as it comes to fetish wear.
  • Sexy underwear makes you feel beautiful.

2: Shopping

  • Keep a list of gifts you may want to receive (including experiences, theatre shows, exhibitions…)
  • Don’t buy things. You don’t need them.
  • Buy things for others only if you know they need them.
  • Choose hand-made gifts or spending time with others over buying items.

Chapter Seven: Fears

Anxieties, worries and fears.

  • In my life time climate change will force me to fight for basic resources.
  • I will never have enough money for it to stop being a big fear preventing me from working on my personal projects.
  • I will never achieve anything of value, or produce anything of importance.
  • It will turn out I am an impostor, indeed living in a little fantasy of having some special talents and qualities.
  • Mum will die and I will not know what to do.

Chapter Eight: Aspirations

  • Always do more listening than speaking.
  • Start to invest.
  • Learn a more maths/science driven skill (coding, machine learning).
  • Start an own side business that would provide at least a little passive income.
  • Do more art: poetry, collages, other projects/items.
  • Start training a combat sport — Muai Thai?
  • Play drums on stage.
