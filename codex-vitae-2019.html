Chapter One: Philosophy

Beliefs, principles and political values that guide everything else in the codex.

1: Meta-beliefs

  • Personal growth comes from understanding that beliefs are fluid. We need evidence to generate accurate beliefs. Not resisting new evidence leads to personal growth.
  • Holding on to axioms leads to close-mindedness and prejudice. Keep your identity small to make it easier to adjust faced with new evidence.
  • Your beliefs shouldn’t reflect what sort of person you are, but how the world is.

2: Personal Principles

  • Freedom is happiness. Ability to always pick what you want to work on, rather than being stuck in what you have to do, gives a sense of self-direction and increases your leverage. Maintain your freedom.
  • Doing good. You are lucky to live in the 5% of the world with highest income. It is your duty to give back.
  • Compound interest. Small actions compound over time. This applies to relationships, learning, and business. If you don’t intend to work to improve something, at least don’t make it worse.
  • Decisions are tiring. Reducing decision making costs gives more bandwidth.
  • Cognitive power is the only currency. Direct it towards what matters. Delighting in your own problems is a form of self-indulgence.
  • Courage is to do the right thing when no one is watching. To stick to the path you know is right even if it doesn't promise any future recognition.
  • Life of meaning demands courage. It's one in which your values will be tested, extended, and expressed.
  • Practice being uncomfortable. Leave your comfort zone regularly. Overcoming small challenges gives perspective and helps focus on more important issues.
  • Don’t focus on things you can’t control. Learn more from the Stoics. Don't make your feelings dependent on external metrics, but on living in accordance with your values.
  • Don’t negotiate with yourself. Be the person who you strive to become; resist doing things that are easy, but violate your principles.

3: Politics and Society

  • The right to an opinion doesn’t make an opinion valid. Encroaching on other people’s rights and wellbeing can’t be excused by following an opinion or chosen lifestyle.
  • Religious groups/ religion are consistently slowing down the progress of humanity— fossilising our views on ethics and actively harming people (physically and mentally).
  • Decentralised systems and technologies are the next step to uninhibited global collaboration and a new framework of trust. Relying on human-lead systems is unscalable.
  • Nothing is binary and labelling people leads to prejudice. Always opt to hear a longer explanation about someone’s views rather than stick to a label (ask “what does it mean for you to be a feminist/vegan/anti-natalist/EA?”)
  • Don't assume people know the definitions of concepts that are dear to you (feminism, veganism, EA). Be sure you know how to explain them without impatience.
  • You will likely not convince people of anything in one conversation. Focus on asking questions about the reasons of their beliefs, and at least not alienating them against the concepts you stand for.

Chapter Two: Personal Systems

Systems and tactics for getting the most out of your life.

1: Productivity

  • Always incorporate sport or physical activity into you day.
  • Start a working session, by listing 1-3 main tasks you want to accomplish.
  • Always get out of the way first the thing you least want to do.
  • Give yourself permission to start and not finish a task rather than postpone it.
  • List random distracting thoughts, and task on a piece of paper. Complete them in bulk, after a working session.
  • Have a clear plan for every large task: break it down into small pieces. If you feel a lot of resistance against starting it, it's probably because it's not broken down well.
  • Have an end time to your tasks (I will work on this until Xpm); and to your work day.
  • When possible: Work not where you live, and definitely not in the same room you sleep in.
  • Keep your workspace quiet. You have the right to tell other people to turn their phones/computers down especially in shared work spaces.

2: Food

  • Don’t waste time on food decisions. Eat the same thing every day. If others don't cook for you, cook simple things in bulk.
  • Always carry some food with you when you travel. there is no guarantee you'll find anything you can/want to eat in transit. Or that you'd have decision bandwidth for that.
  • Keep easy-to-eat, protein-filled food at hand for sudden hunger (tofu, cans of chickpeas, or beans work well). Keep fruits or heathy snacks around always for evening hunger.
  • Everyone should strive to reduce their meat consumption. Encouraging reducetarianism in more people is more efficient that convincing one to be vegan. Lead by example.
  • Having a set time for an evening meal makes it easier to structure the day and prevent overeating.
  • Meals with people are a learned skill. Can be stressful, but as a habit can also teach to eat "normally”/not overeat (comparison with others). You can still set boundaries to how long they will last. Don't force yourself if it causes too much distress.
  • Stop before eating and think about your reasons. Are you really hungry or are there other reasons ( pleasure, socializing, habit, boredom, sadness, etc.)? Ask your future self how thy feel about this decision?
  • Drink a lot of green tea or water during the day. Always keep a full cup/bottle at your desk. Otherwise you'll forget, and this keeps you more focused.

3: Emotional Health

  • Meditate to control your inner life and separate yourself form your thoughts and feelings.
  • Make time for new experiences: art, new activities, new body sensations. It triggers new thoughts and slows time down (retrospectively).
  • Practice gratitude.
    • Notice the positive impact of others on your life and let them know in words or actions.
    • Noticing the lack of suffering is a good way to be grateful in the moment.
  • Find things you can thank others for. Positive feelings should not be kept inside: spreading happiness makes you happier.
  • Do not ignore social life. Interacting with other people, even if not 100% value aligned ones, is necessary to maintain mental stability.
  • Travel always makes you feel more alive, teaches you new things, and prevents getting complacent. If you feel afraid of leaving a place, it means it's time to go.
  • Eating is hard for you. There are many stories in your head that food triggers. Remind yourself these are just stories.

Chapter Three: Work

Lessons for business, work, and building things.

1: Career

  • Learn Data science.
    • In 10 years you would have regretted not doing it now. It's likely to keep you safe in the future job market.
    • When learning gets tough, it's when most people drop. This is why this skill is valued. The ability to persevere makes it the skill for you, not any built-in talent you might have.
    • It could allow you do to earning to give at the same time getting a comfortable life quality.
  • Periodically question the career path you're on. Remind yourself why it's important, readjust if needed. It's easier to prevent doubts from arising rather than dissipate them once they exist.
  • Have a clear(ish) career plan. Don’t jump at every job opportunity you are given. You have enough skills to be choosy.
  • While not a career goal, money is necessary to live. Don't deceive yourself, or use false modesty to excuse getting a low salary or justify not earning enough.

2: Business

  • Pick a job/jobs that you enjoy day to day. Day to day is what most of the work time is comprised of. Don't blindly follow any path.
  • Make sure the tasks you do in the day to day help you develop skills you want to or need to learn, for yourself or for the future career.
  • Suffering in your job just for the sake of the feeling of overcoming hardship doesn't add value to your tasks. No one pays for how you feel while doing the job just for the job done.
  • Don't be afraid to have a side project. It doesn't need to bring money (beware of the myth of the hustle!), it keeps you engaged.

3: Management (of yourself and others)

  • Optimize for increased bandwidth and focus. Pay for it more if necessary.
    • A quiet working space is a must. Coworking with silence or a separate room in an apartment. Don't assume you can work from cafes.
  • Outsourcing dichotomy:
    • Do tasks you enjoy or want to learn how to do, outsource the rest.
    • Outsource what you know how to do best — you’ll be able to asses the results. Focus on things you don’t know exactly how to do.
  • Recognise and avoid fake work.
    • Most meetings are useless. Everything can be summarised in a well-written email. Skype calls should be kept to max 30 min.
    • Email checking and networking are also fake work.
  • Give people the benefit of doubt. If clients or colleagues annoy you, thank them first for what they did right and how they contributed positively. It makes for better relationships.
  • Keep and update a goals list. Even if you don't follow it 100%, it shows you what you considered important month by month (you will forget otherwise). It's also a clear record of your planning fallacy.

4: Skill Development

  • Write more, it’s a skill you need to practice. Ask experienced others to edit your work.
  • Do most creative an mind-axing work in the morning, before you start work.
  • Assign time to reading non-fiction, otherwise it won't happen. Books, papers & long format are equally mind-taxing, don't expect yourself to be able to read all of them at the same time.
  • Do a little bit of programming/learning DS every day. It's very easy to forget skills and lose the habit.

Chapter Four: People

Beliefs and practices for other people.

1: Strangers

  • Everyday we're surrounded by a parade of insecurities. Leave people happier/better off after taking to you than when they started. It might require not saying everything you'd like to.
  • Always be the polite and understanding person you perceive yourself as. Leaving a conversation on a good note, leaves you more peaceful.
  • Use NVC. If you find something uncomfortable, rude, annoying start by stating the facts.
    • Call out men if they catcall or whistle. “What is it that you said? I find it rude. Please, don’t do it again.”
  • Most people don't care about the truth. You're unlikely to convince them about anything. The best you can do it to bring them one step closer, or at least not alienate towards the concept or other people with your beliefs.

2: Friends

  • Most people will not understand you. Cherish those who you share something with.
    • Know who your close friends are and invest in them. At the least, send an occasional “what’s new?” message.
    • Invest only in relationships that make you feel positive. Make time for long chats with Oliver.
  • It’ll always be worth travelling to see a friend.
  • Send cards for Christmas or without an occasion.
  • Never hesitate to ask friends for help. You chose them for shared values and intelligence; collectively they have more expertise than you do.

3: Family

  • Try not to get wound up by them. Only comment on contentious issues when explicitly asked.
  • Encourage a non-material gift culture: experiences, time, or commitment to something are more valuable than items.
  • Can surprise you with their attitudes, give them a chance. When in good mood, try an intelligent discussion like you have with friends.
  • Don’t force yourself to be kind, if they do something you wouldn’t let fly if it was a friend or a stranger.
  • Spend time with mum. You have less time left to spend together than you want to admit to yourself. She deserves your time and to be taken out.

4: Partnership

  • Spend time with people who have qualities you want to improve. Not with those who will enhance the qualities you want to fight. Non-judgemental, open, perceptive, self-aware people are your target.
  • No one person can provide you all that you need emotionally, and it’s ok. Likewise you don't have to be ok with all their behaviours. Even the best partners will annoy you sometimes.
  • Be clear about what you want from any partner and what you are happy to give them. Establish clear boundaries early.
  • Resist the temptation to be a mother or personal-coach to people. It will consume you and destroy your individuality. Note when you have this response and reject it: it’s not your problem, it’s not your job.
  • There should not be a topic you feel you can’t talk about with your partner.
  • Accept that some people will simply like you for who you are. It doesn’t mean immediate dependency, or requires immediate reciprocity.

5: Sex

  • You don’t owe sex, or any part of the act, to anyone.
  • It’s not a performance. Try not to focus on how you look, but on the sensations.
  • Physical contact is a way to deal with insecurities and release tension. Don’t fear it.
  • Sex is an activity/hobby like any other. It doesn’t have any extra deep/relationship meaning, unless you ascribe it to it.
  • Avoid using sex or other people as a tool to boosting self-confidence.

Chapter Five: Nitty Gritty

Important habits to take care of the details of life.

1: Clothes

  • Reduce number of clothing items. There is always excess.
  • If buy anything, invest in quality, comfort, durability.

2: Shopping & Gifts

  • Keep a list of gifts you may want to receive (including experiences, theatre shows, exhibitions…). Makes it easier to delegate gifts when an occasion comes.
  • Don’t buy things. You don’t need them. Buy things for others only if you know they need them.
  • Choose hand-made gifts or spending time with others over buying items.

3: Apartments

  • Invest in a high quality comfort home. You need a refuge after a day of dealing with reality, and you have too many anxiety triggers to compromise on this part financially.
  • Bedroom needs to be quiet and have thick curtains.
  • Proximity to nature and/or ability to take comfortable walks is preferable.
  • Co-livings are great. When choosing co-workings pay attention to noise levels.

Chapter Seven: Anxieties, worries and fears.

  • In my lifetime climate change will force me to fight for basic resources, or a pandemic will turn life into a fight for survival.
  • I will never have enough money for it to stop being a big fear preventing me from working on my personal projects.
  • I will never achieve anything of value, or produce anything of importance.
  • It will turn out I am an impostor, indeed living in a little fantasy of having some special talents and qualities.
  • Mum will die and I will not know what to do, regret not spending enough time with her.
  • Years of neglecting my ED or my bone/posture problems will result in a serious disease that will make my or others' life unbearable.
  • Tom will die and I'll have to readjust to being lonely.
